# Chronic-Kidney-Disease

Summary overview of the meaning of all of those columns:

a) Edema, also spelled oedema, is known as fluid retention, swelling, dropsy and hydropsy, is the build-up of fluid in the body's tissue.<br> Most commonly, the legs or arms are affected.

b) Anemia is when you have low levels of healthy red blood cells to carry oxygen throughout your body.

c) Albumin is a family of globular proteins.Albumin with 0 level is not real. The lowest normal albumin range for adults typically starts around 3.4 to 3.5 grams per deciliter (g/dL).<br>
Albumin levels below that level indicating hypoalbuminemia (low albumin), a sign of potential issues like liver disease, kidney problems, <br>malnutrition, inflammation, or severe illness

d) Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) occurs when plaque (fatty deposits) builds up in the heart's arteries (atherosclerosis), narrowing them and reducing blood, oxygen, and nutrient flow to the heart muscle, leading to chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, and potentially heart attack or heart failure, managed through lifestyle changes, medications, and procedures. 

e) Diabetes mellitus, commonly known as diabetes, is a group of common endocrine diseases characterized by sustained high blood sugar levels. Diabetes is due to either the pancreas not producing enough of the hormone insulin

f) Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a common condition where the force of blood against artery walls is consistently too high, making the heart work harder and increasing the risk for heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, and dementia.

g) Red blood cells or erythrocytes, are the most common blood cells, made in the bone marrow, containing hemoglobin, and responsible for carrying oxygen from lungs to body tissues and returning carbon dioxide for removal.

i) Pus cells are essentially dead white blood cells (leukocytes) that build up with tissue fluid, dead cells, and bacteria at the site of an infection, forming pus, a thick, often yellowish fluid seen in wounds or abscesses.

j) Pus cell clumps are clusters of dead white blood cells, dead germs, and tissue debris, forming a thick fluid (pus) when the body fights an infection, common in abscesses, pimples, or urinary tract issues (pyuria).

k) Serum creatinine is a blood test measuring creatinine, a muscle waste product, to check how well your kidneys filter blood. Higher levels: Indicate kidneys aren't filtering efficiently, suggesting kidney dysfunction or disease.

l) Sodium helps control water levels, nerve signals, and muscle function.

m) Potassium is an essential mineral and electrolyte vital for nerve signals, muscle contractions (including the heart's rhythm), fluid balance, and regulating blood pressure by counteracting sodium

n) Hemoglobin is a protein containing iron that facilitates the transportation of oxygen in red blood cells.

o) Packed Cell Volume also known as hematocrit is a crucial blood test measuring the percentage of red blood cells (RBCs) in your total blood, reflecting its oxygen-carrying capacity and used to diagnose anemia (low PCV), dehydration (high PCV), or excess RBCs (polycythemia) by spinning blood to separate cells from plasma.

p) White blood cells are crucial immune system cells made in the bone marrow that defend the body against infection, disease, and foreign invaders like bacteria and viruses by destroying harmful substances, producing antibodies, and engulfing debris.

q) Specific gravity on a blood test measures blood density relative to water, indicating hydration and kidney function, with normal ranges around 1.048-1.066 for blood, but it's most commonly tested in urine (1.005-1.030), showing kidney's ability to concentrate urine, reflecting hydration, diabetes, or heart issues, with high levels meaning concentrated (dehydration, glucose) and low levels meaning dilute (overhydration, diabetes insipidus). 

r) A random blood glucose test measures blood sugar at any time, without fasting, to screen for diabetes.
